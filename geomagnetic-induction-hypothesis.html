The Geomagnetic Induction Hypothesis

A possible explanation for localized melting and vitrification of ancient masonry.

Proposed by Paul Duke — November 2026

The Observation

Ancient structures around the world contain examples of melted, fused, or vitrified brick and stone. Some locations exhibit extraordinary heating while others appear relatively unaffected.

Conventional fires and deliberate burning explain some documented examples. But could an unusual natural electrical phenomenon account for others?

The Hypothesis

An exceptionally rapid and extreme change in Earth's magnetic field, possibly involving a polarity reversal, could induce powerful electrical currents within the Earth's crust.

Differences in geological and material conductivity might concentrate electrical energy in certain locations, producing intense localized heating, electrical discharges, and possibly melting. Nearby areas could remain comparatively unaffected.

A Possible Mechanism

  1. Magnetic instability: Earth's magnetic field enters an unusually disturbed state, perhaps developing multiple shifting poles.
  2. Rapid transition: A major magnetic change occurs over a relatively short interval.
  3. Electromagnetic induction: Rapidly changing magnetic flux generates electric fields and currents in conductive material.
  4. Uneven current distribution: Differences in geology, mineral content, moisture, salts, and material conductivity affect the paths through which electrical currents flow.
  5. Localized heating: Under sufficiently extreme conditions, electrical resistance or dielectric breakdown might produce intense heating in selected locations.

This proposed mechanism does not require an external solar eruption or passing celestial body. The energy would originate from whatever process drives the extraordinary magnetic disturbance.

Why the Damage Might Be Uneven

Electrical conductivity is not uniform across Earth's surface or within ancient masonry. Some formations may offer preferential paths for induced currents, while others resist their flow.

The resulting heating could therefore be highly irregular, potentially accounting for localized vitrification without requiring the entire planet to experience the same damage.

The mechanism could also recur during separate magnetic disturbances, meaning not all melted structures need originate from a single event.

Questions Worth Investigating

  • Could an extraordinarily rapid geomagnetic change generate sufficient electrical energy to melt masonry?
  • Could ancient materials or geological formations concentrate electrical currents?
  • Would electrical heating leave identifiable physical or magnetic signatures?
  • Do unusual vitrified structures exhibit evidence consistent with electrical discharge rather than conventional fire?

This is an untested hypothesis, not a claim of established fact. Known geomagnetic disturbances have not been shown to generate enough surface heating to melt buildings. The purpose of this proposal is to suggest a potential mechanism and invite investigation.
